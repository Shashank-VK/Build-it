## Honest gaps (what could NOT be verified or found)

1. **Primary sources not fetched this pass.** Most framework claims (Torres, Cagan, Blank, Fitzpatrick, Ulwick's books) are represented via community skill docs and summaries, not the original books or svpg.com/producttalk.org pages. Corroboration across independent summaries is good, but verbatim quotes from secondary docs should be treated as attributed, not verified.
2. **CB Insights primary analysis.** The ~42% "no market need" figure is corroborated through multiple citing sources, but the original CB Insights postmortem analyses were not fetched; methodology details (sample, coding of reasons) remain unchecked.
3. **Support-ticket mining (A14).** No primary source verified this pass — marked Inferred. The practice is widespread, but a citable primary reference (e.g., a company engineering blog on support-driven discovery) was not found in this search round.
4. **Design-thinking critique (B9).** No dedicated critical source was verified; marked Inferred from adjacent "theater" critiques.
5. **Quantitative base rates.** Figures like "1 in 3 product ideas create measurable positive results" (cited in B4's source) and "41 of 50 MVPs" (C7) are not audited — recorded here with low confidence and explicit do-not-cite guidance.
6. **AI-in-discovery empirical studies.** The AI pitfalls section (A12–A13) rests on practitioner essays and secondhand citations of institutional research (Pew, The Markup, Oxford Internet Institute), not on fetched primary studies. The *direction* of the warnings is corroborated; the *magnitude* is unknown.
7. **Pricing benchmarks.** Van Westendorp / Gabor-Granger descriptions come from commercial vendors (SurveyKing, Appinio); independent methodological references were not consulted.
8. **Non-English and non-US ecosystems.** All sources are English-language, overwhelmingly US-centric startup/SaaS. Applicability to other markets, regulated industries, and hardware is unexamined — mark Unknown for those contexts.

*End of register.*
