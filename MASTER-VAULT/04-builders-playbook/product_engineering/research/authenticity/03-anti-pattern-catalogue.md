# 03 — Anti-Pattern Catalogue

**The Omniscient Resource Atlas · product_engineering / research / authenticity**
**Built 2026-10-10 · Read-only research. Claim labels: Verified / Corroborated / Inferred / Proposed / Unknown.**

## How to read this catalogue

Each hypothesis below is investigated — **not** asserted — through six questions:

1. **Why generic/unfinished** — the mechanism by which it signals low craft or low specificity.
2. **Pattern vs execution** — whether the problem is the pattern itself or its unexamined use. Default finding, per E04: *execution*, not the pattern.
3. **Evidence/examples** — sources; IDs (E01–E51) resolve in `04-evidence-register.md`.
4. **When appropriate** — legitimate uses, with the rationale required.
5. **Alternatives** — what to do instead.
6. **How to evaluate** — a concrete test.

**Master caution (applies to every row):** these are tripwires, not laws (01 P-06). E04: "do not ban these patterns globally"; E05: a ban list without a design direction produces *sterile* pages that read as generated "just as loudly as a purple gradient." E08 (aesthetic–usability) cuts both ways: beauty can mask real usability defects — passing this catalogue is not passing a usability test. Subjective taste is never stated as universal law; where a row rests on taste, it says so.

## B. Copywriting patterns (14)

| # | Pattern | (1) Why it can look generic / unfinished | (2) Pattern vs execution | (3) Evidence / examples | (4) When appropriate | (5) Alternatives | (6) Evaluation test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Repetitive AI-sounding marketing language | "Transform / Elevate / Unlock" vagueness (E04); averaged tone makes messaging interchangeable and weakens brand recall. | Execution. No word is banned; the failure is *unexamined clustering* — abstraction with no specifics. | E04; E31; E33 | When paired with concrete specifics ("processes 50k events/sec"). | Precise nouns/verbs, named entities, numbers (E31). | Delete the adjective: if the sentence says *more*, keep the deletion. |
| C2 | Unsupported claims ("revolutionary") | "Industry-leading solution evaporates; specific numbers land" (E35); unsubstantiated superlatives erode trust and attract regulatory attention for product claims (E20). | Execution. Superlatives require substantiation — FTC requires substantiation of product claims (E20). | E35; E20; E39 | When substantiated: awards, benchmarks, "first to X" with evidence one click away. | Specific, verifiable statements. | Evidence test: can the proof be reached within one click? ## E. Using the catalogue without turning it into law

1. **Tripwire, then rationale.** Flag the pattern → answer (2) honestly → either justify in writing (the E04 audit format: severity, certainty, why it reads generic, product justification, correction) or change it.
2. **Direction before deletion.** Removing flagged patterns without a Design DNA yields the sterile mean (01 P-08) — equally recognizable as generated.
3. **Counterexamples are load-bearing.** File 02 V-05 exists so this catalogue cannot harden into a ban list. When a flagged pattern is the right call, the catalogue's (4) column should confirm it.
4. **Catalogue ≠ usability test.** Per E08, a beautiful page that passes every row can still be unusable; behaviour testing is a separate obligation.
