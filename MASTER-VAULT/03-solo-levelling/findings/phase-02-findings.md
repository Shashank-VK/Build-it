Verification pass: "Amazon narrative meeting silent reading review PR FAQ" — confirmed by multiple independent practitioner accounts.

## Basecamp/37signals

**Q1 — First artifact and its name:**
Claim: The Pitch is the named first formal artifact. Written during the "shaping" phase by whoever does the shaping (typically a senior strategist or Head of Product), NOT by the team that will build it. Five required sections: Problem, Appetite, Solution, Rabbit Holes, No-Gos.
Search queries used: "Basecamp Shape Up pitch document required sections appetite evidence customer problem betting table"
Source: *Shape Up* (Ryan Singer, Basecamp, 2019) — Chapter 2, freely available at basecamp.com/shapeup.
Verification pass: "Basecamp Shape Up pitch five sections Ryan Singer" — directly confirmed by basecamp.com/shapeup. Confirmed.
Tag: [PRIMARY]

**Q2 — Evidence bar required:**
Claim: The Problem section must describe a real use case or specific customer pain point — with evidence from customer feedback, support tickets, or observed behavior where available. No specific quantitative threshold required, but it must be a real problem, not hypothetical.
Search queries used: "Basecamp Shape Up pitch problem section evidence required customer pain"
Source: *Shape Up* (Ryan Singer, 2019).
Verification pass: Confirmed via basecamp.com/shapeup.
Tag: [PRIMARY]

**Q3 — Reviewer(s):**
Claim: The Betting Table is the review mechanism — a small group of senior decision-makers (CEO, CTO, and/or senior technical and product leads). Meets at end of each cool-down period (every 8 weeks: 6-week cycle + 2-week cool-down). Only shaped pitches are reviewed — no backlog.
Search queries used: "Basecamp Shape Up betting table who attends how often review pitches"
Source: *Shape Up* (Ryan Singer, 2019).
Verification pass: Confirmed via basecamp.com/shapeup and 37signals.com posts.
Tag: [PRIMARY]

**Q4 — Kill authority:**
Claim: The Betting Table holds kill authority. A non-selected pitch is NOT added to a backlog — it is let go entirely. If someone still believes in it, they must re-pitch it in a future cycle. Direct quote from Shape Up: "If we can't bet on it now, we let it go."
Search queries used: "Basecamp Shape Up betting table pitch rejected no backlog let go"
Source: *Shape Up* (Ryan Singer, 2019) — explicit statement.
Verification pass: Confirmed via basecamp.com/shapeup direct quote.
Tag: [PRIMARY]

**Q5 — Reversibility classification (Lens 1):**
Claim: Betting Table decision is reversible — a rejected pitch can be re-proposed in the next cycle. However, once a bet is placed within a cycle, the decision is treated as a firm commitment — the 6 weeks are not rescinded mid-cycle.
Search queries used: "Basecamp Shape Up bet reversed mid-cycle commitment firm"
Source: *Shape Up* describes the 6-week cycle as a firm commitment to protect teams from interruption.
Verification pass: Confirmed via basecamp.com/shapeup.
Tag: [PRIMARY]

**Q6 — Process cost (Lens 2):**
Claim: Pitch document is intentionally lightweight. Shaping is done by 1–2 people. Betting Table is a small group meeting covering multiple pitches in one session during the 2-week cool-down. Total cycle: 8 weeks (6 build + 2 cool-down).
Search queries used: "Basecamp Shape Up shaping time cost betting table duration effort"
Source: *Shape Up* — 6+2 cycle structure explicitly stated.
Verification pass: Confirmed via basecamp.com/shapeup.
Tag: [PRIMARY]

**Q7 — Rejection record-keeping:**
Claim: Explicitly NO formal record-keeping by design. When a pitch is not selected, it is let go with no entry into a backlog, log, or archive. Direct quote: "We don't keep a backlog."
Search queries used: "Basecamp Shape Up rejected pitch no backlog no record kept"
Source: *Shape Up* (Ryan Singer, 2019) — direct quote.
Verification pass: Confirmed via basecamp.com/shapeup.
