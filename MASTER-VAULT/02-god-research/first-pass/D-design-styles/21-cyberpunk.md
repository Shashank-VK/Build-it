# 21 — Cyberpunk

## Meaning
Cyberpunk as a design style = visual language of "high tech, low life": a futuristic, high-technology urban future inhabited by oppressed / outsider populations under corporate / systemic control. Origin in New Wave sci-fi literature (Philip K. Dick `Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?` 1968, William Gibson `Neuromancer` 1984) and films `Blade Runner` (1982), `Akira` (1988), `The Matrix` (1999). In design it functions both as worldbuilding aesthetic and as ideology-springboard (rebellion, anti-establishment, transhumanism).

## Elements
- Palette: near-black / dirty blue-gray grounds + toxic neon accents: cyan, magenta/pink, acid green, warning red, purple-blue; high contrast, low-key lighting
- Imagery: dense megacity, skyscrapers, rain-lit alleys, billboards/screens, cables/pipes/exposed infrastructure, drones, augmented / cybernetic faces, circuitry, surveillance HUD
- Typography / graphics: condensed variable / monospace all-caps with wide tracking, clipped corners, hazard stripes, serial numbers; glitch effects, scanlines, RGB separation, flicker, distortion, digital noise, grainy texture
- Layout: dense, stacked panes, off-axis overlays, unconventional / rule-breaking composition; bright priority channel to separate actionable data from atmospheric noise
- Mood: noir, gritty, dystopian, mysterious, unsettling; artificial light as primary mood-setter + haze/smog

## Uses
- Games, sci-fi film / anime key art, book covers (airbrushed bold schemes), music posters, nightlife branding
- Security tools, crypto products, tech / `Wired`-type editorial covers, AR / HUD / FUI concepts, streetwear / techwear
- UI themes (dark dystopian surfaces), logo / poster experiments with clashing neons and glitch

## Overlap note — preserve terminology
- vs Synthwave: both use 1980s neon. Sources explicitly contrast: Synthwave = sincere, nostalgic sunset / coastal road / hopeful future; Cyberpunk = noir, dystopian night-city. Do not merge.
- vs Cybercore: see 24-cybercore.md for two competing Cybercore definitions. Cyberpunk = cinematic worldbuilding scope (city/corporation/dystopia); hacker-terminal Cybercore = personal / lo-fi insider scale.
