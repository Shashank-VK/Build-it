# 23 — Victorian

## Meaning
Victorian / Victoriana in graphic design = maximalist commercial-print language of Queen Victoria's reign (1837-1901), revived as vintage / retro style. Philosophy "more-is-more": text as ornament, eclecticism, exoticism, horror vacui (fear of empty space). Reacted to industrial / scientific rigidity with vibrancy; enabled by wood engraving, lithography, chromolithography.

## Elements
- Typography: wide range mixed on same page — fat faces (Elephant, Columbian), slab serifs (Clarendon/Egyptian), ornamental / floral display faces, condensed titling faces, Gothic / Celtic Revival faces, circus-style; multiple typefaces/sizes/weights in one composition; decorative initials / drop caps
- Ornament: borders, frames, fleurons / printers' flowers, vignettes, arabesques (Islamic-inspired interlace), floral motifs (naturalistic to abstract), geometric guilloche / tessellation, medallions/badges, scrollwork/filigree, corner ornaments
- Layout: central-axis symmetrical, header-heavy, stacked vertical bands, ornamental rules/dividers as structure; dense but organized hierarchy via size/weight
- Material / color: aged paper / linen / leather / wood textures; burgundy, gold, forest green, ivory; sharp rectangular forms, not rounded/minimal; aged, rich, not flat bright modern

## Uses
- Theatre posters, playbills, broadsheets, timetables, billheads, sheet-music covers, packaging, bookplates, signage
- Contemporary revival: packaging, poster, editorial headlines, brand identities seeking tradition / authority / craftsmanship; vintage design; precursor to Industrial / Steampunk / Hipster styling

## Overlap note
- vs Gothic (27): Victorian includes Gothic Revival typography (pointed arches, trefoils) as one eclectic source among many; Gothic design style is the broader medieval-rooted system. Preserve both terms; do not collapse Victorian maximalism into Gothic darkness.
